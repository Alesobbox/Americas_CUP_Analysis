# Learning routes for independent understanding

This pack is about the actual dirty working state reviewed on 4 November 2026. Start with the textbook and use the source companion when a claim becomes interesting or doubtful. The source companion is deliberately selective; the excluded raw archive and prepared forecasting caches are operational inputs, not prerequisites for reading. Every executable concept example is labelled synthetic. Actual race evidence is stored separately in `evidence/`.

## One hour orientation

Spend 10 minutes with the field guide's system page and the textbook's sailing primer. Say aloud how a gate differs from a mark and why speed differs from progress. Spend 15 minutes on the evidence ladder and the three race case studies. Name one measured quantity, one reconstructed quantity, one interpolated state and one unavailable quantity. Spend 15 minutes on performance indexes: compute the synthetic 150 index and the different 125 ratio of means. Spend 10 minutes on the three forecast targets and Cup bounds. Finish with 10 minutes on the trust chapter and a closed-book explanation: “What can this publication establish, and what extra evidence would change my view?”

If one hour is all you have, do not spend it reading code from top to bottom. Follow one reported number from interface to export to producing function and return to the question it answers.

## One day conceptual review

Morning session 1, 75 minutes: Parts I-II. Draw the course, clocks and evidence ladder from memory. Check the archive table and explain the 227/224 distinction. Break for 15 minutes. Morning session 2, 90 minutes: Part III, then workbook questions 1-12. Derive shortest-arc interpolation and crossing fractions on paper. Inspect the matching source lines only after predicting what they must do.

Afternoon session 1, 90 minutes: Part IV and the actual fleet/match cases. Work the peer-baseline questions without looking at solutions. Break for 15 minutes. Afternoon session 2, 90 minutes: Part V. Explain nonfinish scoring and why unresolved Cup intervals cannot be normalized. Finish with 45 minutes on Parts VI-VIII, five interface journeys and the first two proposed experiments. Record three claims you can defend and three you still cannot.

## Three day deeper route

Day 1 follows the measurement chain. Read Parts I-III, inspect identity, projection, resampling, reconstruction and manoeuvre modules in that order, then compare them with exported provenance. Work beginner and intermediate geometry questions. End the day with an oral explanation of a misleading apparent crossing. Deliverable: a one-page drawing of clocks, coordinate systems and missing-data boundaries.

Day 2 follows the inference chain. Read Part IV, inspect the historical producer and performance tests, then read Part V and causal feature/evaluation code. Run the standard-library concept lab if Python is available; otherwise read its saved output. Work forecasting and critique questions. Deliverable: a short defense of index 110 and a refusal to make two unsupported claims from it.

Day 3 follows the interaction and trust chains. Read Parts VI-VIII and the atlas; inspect App, the scenario service and the artifact validator. Revisit the case studies and solve the debugging scenarios. Draft one falsifiable experiment with a fixed baseline, evaluation unit, success and failure criteria. Deliverable: rehearse the narration in your own words, then teach the project to someone who has never seen it.

## Dependency aware source reading order

First read the compact exported schemas and one bounded case. Then study identity and projection; interpolation depends on those conventions. Study crossing and leg reconstruction before elapsed times and VMG. Study coverage eligibility before aggregates. Study per-leg performance before overall radar profiles. Study scheduled fields and causal clocks before rankings, then completion and correlated simulations before event tournaments. Study artifact contracts before the local generation service; its job is to publish a valid result, not to fit inside the browser. Study shared App state before individual panels so that selection, visibility and comparison do not become three unrelated concepts.

The atlas lists subsystem status and tests. Read active paths first. Experimental alternatives and retained compatibility branches make more sense once the current publication path is clear.

## Questions to take on a walk

1. Which extra observation would most reduce uncertainty in the next decision: a missing gate time, an independently verified finish, a new race-day or AC75 data?
2. Could a boat improve its speed index while worsening its result? Construct a course and denominator explanation.
3. What makes a causal feature causal: its timestamp, its publication time, or the filtering rule that checks both?
4. How should missingness be displayed when replay motion is smooth but historical coverage fails?
5. What would make the simplest progress-based live baseline sufficient?
6. What kind of evidence would identify a wind-direction response separately from venue and crew?
7. How could persistent series strength change a tournament even when every individual match has the same marginal win probability?
8. If official rules resolved every tie, which uncertainties would remain?
9. Which unavailable number protects the reader from a false conclusion?
10. What result would make you abandon a preferred improvement proposal?
