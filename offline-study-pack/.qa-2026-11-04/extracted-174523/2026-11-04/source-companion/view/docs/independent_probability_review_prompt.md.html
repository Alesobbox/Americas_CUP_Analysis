3You are an independent reviewer with expertise in statistical forecasting, Bayesian inference, ranking models, stochastic simulation, machine learning, sailing dynamics and production software. 9## 1. Product objective: exactly three user-facing outputs
10
11The user wants:
12
131. **Live race-win probability:** for each boat at replay time t, the probability of being the first valid on-water finisher, given information actually available by t, conditions, position on the course, opponents and eligible history.
142. **Pre-race expected finishing position:** for each scheduled boat, its expected finishing position before the gun, given the opponents, historical relative strength and recorded or explicitly entered conditions. This is an expectation derived from an outcome distribution, not itself a probability.
153. **Whole-event win probability:** the probability of winning the named event after accounting for its actual format, scores, remaining races and dependencies. The current application's third card targets the **2027 America's Cup, raced in AC75s**, using an archive of **2026 AC40 sessions**. A 2026 preliminary regatta and the 2027 Cup are different targets. Verify that this target matches the code and intended product; do not silently substitute an easier event.
16
17Write the estimands mathematically, including the information set, eligible field, race status, conditioning events and scoring convention. Distinguish first valid on-water finish from official classification after protests or later disqualifications. Ask a focused clarification only if an irreducible target ambiguity materially affects implementation; continue independent work meanwhile.
18
19The existing expected-position convention assigns nonfinishers the scheduled field size N, so several boats may receive N. Check whether this is appropriate and explain alternatives before changing it. Under tied-N scoring, expected positions need not sum to N(N+1)/2. A boat can have the highest win probability and a worse expected position than another boat in a perfectly coherent joint distribution. Do not introduce either false constraint.
20
21Keep exactly these three product outputs. Internal finish/nonfinish risks, rank distributions, latent states, no-valid-finisher mass and uncertainty diagnostics may be necessary. They do not authorize additional probability cards or a silent change to a conditional-on-finishing target.

102## 5. Audit questions
103
104### A. Targets, labels, identity and effective sample size
105
106Reconcile source entries, replay entries, teams, A/B crews, asset IDs and their effective-time mappings. Do not merge distinct crews without a model or discard boats lacking telemetry. Determine which unit is being predicted and which unit shares historical strength.
107
108Audit practice versus competition, source flags versus geometry, reconstructed results versus independent evidence, DNS/DNF/RET/OCS/DSQ, censoring, shortened races and ambiguous completion. Distinguish recording failure from actual retirement. Include scheduled entries in the correct denominator.
109
110Evaluate label fitness for the actual on-water estimand. Independent official classification is valuable but is not automatically the only possible ground truth for a first-on-water target. Conversely, telemetry-derived order is not automatically verified truth. Establish what can be supported, reviewed, bounded or estimated under explicit assumptions.
111
112Count independent races, dates, crews and conditions. Millions of correlated frames are not millions of independent outcome labels. Examine label uncertainty, selection effects and exclusion coverage.
113
114### B. Causality and time availability
115
116Trace source event time, original order, modeled receipt/availability, closed buckets, state time and publication time. Inspect the six-second modeled reorder buffer, out-of-order messages and the distinction between real receipt clocks and event-time reconstruction.
117
118Test that modifying or deleting future telemetry, course changes, statuses, wind, final results or final metadata cannot change earlier predictions. Inspect corrections arriving late, last-observation holds, backward smoothing, interpolation, feature scaling and training cutoffs. All fitted preprocessing must use only eligible training history.
119
120Check source-prefix equivalence, race/day boundaries and the gun-time display. An estimate appearing only after a terminal outcome is not useful pre-outcome forecasting. Retrospective reanalysis, modelled header temperatures and realised future wind must not be passed off as information known at the historical prediction cutoff.
121
122### C. Generative model and mathematical coherence
123
124Reconstruct the actual fitted model and how an observation changes its conditional distribution. Explain whether Monte Carlo is integrating a learned model, a hand-set stochastic model or a mixture. More simulation draws reduce numerical error; they do not establish model accuracy.
125
126Audit ranking likelihoods, identifiability, regularization, pooling, recency, unseen entrants, changing crews and field-size effects. Determine whether weather, course position, velocity, opponents, geometry and remaining distance actually influence the published outputs.
127
128Audit positive leg/finish times, leg-specific lengths, start-loss accounting, current-leg versus future-leg conditioning, manoeuvres, severe performance losses, wind sensitivity, finish constraints and near-finish behavior. Verify dimensions and avoid counting the same uncertainty or delay twice.
129
130Separate exogenous failure, DNS, time-limit censoring and severe but recoverable performance loss. Condition correctly on survival and confirmed starts. Examine whether sparse failure data justify pooled priors, hierarchical shrinkage, sensitivity bounds, conditional estimates or abstention. Challenge overly restrictive availability gates as well as unsupported availability. Do not silently assume all boats finish to make numbers appear.
131
132Generate race-win and expected-position outputs from compatible joint outcomes. Check total mass including no valid finisher, legal impossibilities, ties, nonfinish scores and terminal constraints. Any numerical tail regularizer must preserve impossible events and have a justified scale.
133
134Examine shared weather, persistent ability/form, opponent dependence and temporal dependence. Common factors may cancel in relative rankings or create misleading certainty. Distinguish physical dependence from dependence caused by shared uncertain parameters.
135
136Separate race randomness, state uncertainty, parameter uncertainty, structural uncertainty, class-transfer assumptions and Monte Carlo sampling error. A reported numerical half-width is not automatically a calibrated predictive interval. Check the estimator used to compute it, including any analytic marginalization or weighted mixtures.
137

143- Whether a fixed diffusion-inspired live lead adjustment, including its coefficient and remaining-distance scaling, behaves sensibly and outperforms simple alternatives.
144- Whether covariance factors estimated from in-sample leg-time residuals, fixed shrinkage/caps and the shared weather construction are identifiable or calibrated with this archive.
145- Whether point-estimated strength/time coefficients remain fixed across draws, understating parameter uncertainty.
146- Whether conditional-logit and positive-delay candidates are merely defined rather than connected to fitting, comparison and publication.
147- Whether backtesting substitutes a reference when the nominal candidate is unavailable, obscuring candidate coverage or performance.

153### E. Independent research and model comparison
154
155Search academic papers, official model documentation and relevant primary sporting/forecasting research. Recheck citations in existing documents. Cite direct links, publication/version dates where material, the relevant result and its applicability limits. Clearly distinguish a paper's finding from an inference for this archive. Do not fabricate references or use popularity as performance evidence.
156
157Compare proportionately: uniform/empirical references; Bradley–Terry/Plackett–Luce and conditional/mixed logit; Bayesian hierarchical or dynamic strength; survival/competing-risk and empirical leg-time models; joint Monte Carlo; regularized supervised learning/boosted trees; unsupervised regimes or hidden-state models; sequence neural networks; and Google's TimesFM or other time-series foundation models when relevant.
158
159For TimesFM, determine the exact version, task, covariate/multivariate support, sampling and uncertainty capabilities from primary documentation. Specify what it would forecast, how that would translate into coherent joint finishing outcomes, and whether the archive can test it fairly. Inspect pretrained-data overlap/cutoff risks. A good speed forecast or plausible trajectory does not establish a good race-win forecast.
160
161For every serious candidate, state its estimand/likelihood, required labels, data sufficiency, leakage risks, uncertainty treatment, dependence assumptions, computational cost and decisive comparison. Unsupervised regimes are potential features, not automatically winner labels. Fit representations and clusters inside training folds.
162

179### G. Whole-event/Cup correctness
180

185Simulate the event through legal state transitions. Do not multiply unconditional race-win marginals or assume future races independent.
