Solution 1 — WGS84 Local Tangent Plane Projection [ACTUAL REPOSITORY EVIDENCE]

  1. Local Cartesian Coordinates: Using pipeline/projection.py:L34-L35:
    x = (λ - λ0) · klon = (14.24912086 - 14.23726898) × 84,374.839 = 0.01185188 × 84,374.839 = +1000.00m
    y = (ϕ - ϕ0) · klat = (40.81848636 - 40.80948067) × 111,047.746 = 0.00900569 × 111,047.746 = +1000.06m
  2. Why klon < klat and Cagliari vs Naples: Meridians converge toward the poles by a factor of cosϕ0 (modulated slightly by the prime-vertical radius of curvature N(ϕ0)). At Naples (ϕ0 ≈ 40.81^∘), cos(40.8095^∘) ≈ 0.75689, so klon ≈ 0.76 klat. Because Cagliari lies further south (ϕ0 = 39.18^∘), cos(39.18^∘) ≈ 0.77517, making Cagliari's longitude scale factor 86,407.9 - 84,374.8 = +2,033.1m/deg (+2.41%) wider than Naples. Using Naples projection constants on a Cagliari race would compress East–West distances by 2.41%.

Solution 4 — Wind-Referenced vs Mark-Referenced VMG Sign Convention [SYNTHETIC DATA]

  1. Signed TWA:
    TWA = ((45^∘ - 195^∘ + 540^∘) \bmod 360^∘) - 180^∘ = (390^∘ \bmod 360^∘) - 180^∘ = -150.0^∘
  2. Wind-referenced VMG (replay.telemetry.boats[id].vmg):
    VMGwind = 30.0 cos(-150.0^∘) = 30.0 × (-0.866025) = -25.98kt
  3. Mark-referenced VMG (historical_analysis.py Leg 2):
    VMGmark = 30.0 cos(225^∘ - 195^∘) = 30.0 cos(30^∘) = +25.98kt
    Why they differ: Wind-referenced VMG measures velocity toward TWD = 45^∘ (upwind), so sailing downwind away from the wind yields a negative dot product. Historical leg VMG projects velocity onto the unit vector pointing from the windward gate to the leeward gate (225^∘), so progress toward the leeward gate is positive.

Solution 14 — Debugging the USA Start Conflict in naples_pr1 [ACTUAL REPOSITORY EVIDENCE]

  1. The key lookup bug in pipeline/reconstruction.py:L228: In IngestionResult.to_dict() (pipeline/ingest.py:L395-L420), each competitor's start status is serialized under the nested dictionary comp["start_state"] = {"started": ..., "ocs": ..., "dsq": ...}. Solution 15 — Debugging Non-Monotonic Source Leg Timings in 26_09_22_nap_ipr_pr2 [ACTUAL REPOSITORY EVIDENCE]

  1. Fate of Leg 3: _leg_entry_for_boat sets each leg's start time to the previous leg's end time (start_ms = 1790081725693) and end time to Leg 3's gate_time (1790081553791). Because end_ms <= start_ms, Leg 3 has a negative duration (-171.902 s) and is immediately rejected as quality = "unavailable"!
  2. Overlapping clock intervals on Leg 4: When advancing to Leg 4, the loop sets Leg 4's start_ms to Leg 3's timestamp (1790081553791) and end_ms to Leg 4's timestamp (1790081965371). Because 1790081553791 < 1790081725693 (Leg 2's end time), the time window [1790081553791, 1790081725693] (171.902 s) is counted twice — once inside Leg 2 and once inside Leg 4! Thus USA's valid legs 1, 2, and 4 sum to 321.799 + 303.894 + 411.580 = 1037.273s (1036.288 s covered), exceeding the 865.371s gun-to-finish elapsed time by exactly 171.902s.

Solution 17 — Tactical Wind-Shift OLS Slope & Why 0 Projections Fire [ACTUAL REPOSITORY EVIDENCE]

  1. Circular unwrapping before OLS: In pipeline/tactical_insights.py:L65-L88, the circular mean θ of the 120-second window is computed first. Every directional sample θk is unwrapped into a signed offset yk = θ + signedangle(θk - θ) ∈ [θ-180^∘, θ+180^∘), eliminating 0^∘/360^∘ discontinuities before computing standard linear regression β̂1 = ∑ (tk - t)(yk - y)∑ (tk - t)2.
  2. The 7 sequential projection suppression gates (tactical_insights.py:L255-L295):
  3. Window quality is "derived" (≥ 90s coverage and ≥ 10 samples in the 120 s window).
  4. Circular resultant length R ≥ 0.85 (low directional dispersion).
  5. Absolute rate |rate| ≥ 1.5^∘/min (non-trivial trend).
  6. |rate| ≤ 12.0^∘/min (not an erratic sensor spike or boat-wash artifact).
  7. Coefficient of determination R2 ≥ 0.55 (strong linear fit over the 120 s window).
  8. Split-half sign and magnitude consistency: slopes on the first 60 s and second 60 s must have the same sign and neither half-slope may differ from the full slope by more than 50%.
  9. Projected 5-minute shift |Δθ300s| ≤ 35.0^∘. In real race-course wind records (which oscillate continuously with wave/gust cycles every 20–40 seconds), the R2 ≥ 0.55 and split-half consistency gates reject 100% of linear extrapolations (0 / 3,455).

Solution 20 — Plackett–Luce Discrimination Scale κ = 1.8 and Prior σ = 1.2 [ACTUAL REPOSITORY EVIDENCE]

Solution 21 — Why Custom Pre-Race Wind Speeds Return Identical Expected Positions [ACTUAL REPOSITORY EVIDENCE]

Solution 25 — Mathematical Derivation: The Tied-N Expected Rank Sum Identity [MATHEMATICAL DERIVATION]

  1. Realized rank sum proof: Let F = N - M finishers receive ranks 1, 2, …, F, and let M nonfinishers each receive rank N. Notice that in a complete permutation without nonfinishers, the bottom M positions would have received ranks F+1, F+2, …, N, whose sum is:
    ∑k=1M (F + k) = MF + M(M+1)2 = M(N - M) + M(M+1)2 = MN - M(M-1)2
    Under the tied-N rule, those M boats instead receive total rank MN. Replacing the bottom M permutation ranks with MN changes the total sum from N(N+1)2 to:
    ∑i=1N Ri = N(N+1)2 + (MN - (MN - M(M-1)2)) = N(N+1)2 + M(M-1)2 \blacksquare
  2. Expectation: By linearity of expectation over the random number of nonfinishers M ∈ 0, 1, …, N:
    ∑i=1N 𝔼[Ri] = N(N+1)2 + 12𝔼[M(M-1)] = N(N+1)2 + ∑m=2N ℙ(M = m)m(m-1)2
  3. Why M=0 and M=1 conserve the sum: When M = 0, 0(-1)2 = 0. When M = 1 (a single boat fails to finish), that boat receives rank N, which is the exact same rank (N) it would have received as the last-place boat in a full permutation (1(0)2 = 0)! Only when M ≥ 2 do multiple boats share rank N instead of N-1, N-2, …, creating a positive excess M(M-1)2 > 0.

Solution 26 — Mathematical Derivation: Analytic Plackett–Luce Expected Rank [MATHEMATICAL DERIVATION]

  1. Closed-form expected rank: For boat i, let Ci ∈ 0, 1 be its completion indicator (ℙ(Ci = 1) = si). - If Ci = 0 (probability 1 - si), boat i receives rank Ri = N. - If Ci = 1 (probability si), boat i's rank among the finishers is 1 plus the number of other boats j ≠ i that both complete (Cj = 1) and finish ahead of i (Tj < Ti):
    Ri \big|Ci = 1 = 1 + ∑j ≠ i Cj · 𝕀(Tj < Ti)
    Under Plackett–Luce (equivalently, independent exponential race clocks Ek / wk), conditional on Ci = 1 and Cj = 1, the pairwise probability that j beats i depends only on (wi, wj) by Luce's Choice Axiom: ℙ(Tj < Ti \mid Ci=1, Cj=1) = wjwi + wj. Since Cj is independent of Ci with 𝔼[Cj] = sj, taking expectations yields:
    𝔼[Ri] = N(1 - si) + si (1 + ∑j ≠ i sj wjwi + wj) \blacksquare
  2. Sum when si ≡ 1: When si = 1 for all i, ∑i=1N 𝔼[Ri] = N + ∑i < j (wjwi + wj + wiwi + wj) = N + \binomN2(1) = N + N(N-1)2 = N(N+1)2.

Solution 27 — Counterexample Proof: Highest Win Probability ≠ Best Expected Finish Position [MATHEMATICAL DERIVATION]

  1. Exact calculations: - Boat A: ℙ(win) = 0.45, 𝔼[RA] = 0.45(1) + 0.40(3) + 0.15(3) = 0.45 + 1.20 + 0.45 = 2.10. - Boat B: ℙ(win) = 0.40, 𝔼[RB] = 0.45(2) + 0.40(1) + 0.15(2) = 0.90 + 0.40 + 0.30 = 1.60. - Boat C: ℙ(win) = 0.15, 𝔼[RC] = 0.45(3) + 0.40(2) + 0.15(1) = 1.35 + 0.80 + 0.15 = 2.30. Boat A has the highest win probability (45% > 40%), but Boat B has a substantially better expected finish position (1.60 < 2.10).
  2. Physical sailing interpretation: Boat A is a bimodal "flyer" (e.g., splitting hard to the right boundary looking for a wind shift: if the right shift hits, A wins; if the left shift hits, A finishes last in 3rd). Boat B sails down the middle of the course, covering the fleet: it finishes 1st or 2nd 100% of the time, giving a superior average finish (1.60) despite a slightly lower outright win rate (40%).

Solution 28 — Mathematical Derivation: Contrast Subspace Projection & Pairwise Variance Preservation [MATHEMATICAL DERIVATION]

  1. Projection properties: - Symmetry: P^→p = IK^→p - 1K(1K 1K^→p)^→p = P. - Idempotence: P2 = IK - 2K1K1K^→p + 1K21K(1K^→p 1K)1K^→p = IK - 1K1K1K^→p = P since 1K^→p 1K = K. - Annihilation of 1K: P1K = 1K - KK1K = 0.
  2. Pairwise contrast invariance: For any pair (i, j), let cij = ei - ej. Notice that 1K^→p cij = 1 - 1 = 0, which means cij already lies inside the zero-sum subspace H0, so Pcij = cij! Therefore:
    θ̃i - θ̃j = cij^→p θ̃ = cij^→p P θ = (Pcij)^→p θ = cij^→p θ = θi - θj
    Since the random variable θ̃i - θ̃j is point-for-point identical to θi - θj, its variance Var(θ̃i - θ̃j) = Var(θi - θj) = Σii + Σjj - 2Σij is preserved identically. \blacksquare

Solution 29 — Mathematical Derivation: Boundary Identity Between Pre-Race and Live Checkpoint t = Gun [MATHEMATICAL DERIVATION]

  1. Why naive splitting fails: If a full leg has log-time variance σ2 and a live checkpoint at progress u → 0+ adds an independent remaining-leg residual with full variance σ2 or resamples the crew factor ηcrew, total variance jumps discontinuously at the gun, creating an artificial probability sawtooth at every gate rounding.
  2. Zero-information identity: By holding the persistent crew draw ηcrew and scaling the remaining-leg innovation variance as (1 - u)σ2innov, at u = 0 (zero progress elapsed and no new speed/recovery observation), the remaining-leg distribution has factor (1 - 0)σ2innov = σ2innov and identical mean μ_\ell, reproducing the exact pre-race full-leg distribution in mean, variance, and all quantiles.

Solution 30 — Mathematical Derivation: Persistent vs Independent Ability Draws in First-to-7 Series [MATHEMATICAL DERIVATION]

  1. Persistent tournament-level ability draw: - At p = 0.50: by symmetry, S(0.50) = ∑k=06 \binom6+kk (0.5)7+k = 0.500000. - At p = 0.90: S(0.90) = ∑k=06 \binom6+kk (0.9)7 (0.1)k = 0.999982. - Mixture expectation: 𝔼[S(P)] = 0.5(0.500000) + 0.5(0.999982) = 0.749991 ≈ 75.00%.
  2. Independent per-race ability draw: Averaging P before running the series gives marginal single-race win probability p = 0.5(0.50) + 0.5(0.90) = 0.70. Evaluating the negative binomial sum at p = 0.70:
    S(0.70) = ∑k=06 \binom6+kk (0.7)7 (0.3)k = 0.937615 ≈ 93.76%
    Intuition: Because S(p) is strongly concave on (0.5, 1.0), Jensen's inequality guarantees S(𝔼[P]) > 𝔼[S(P)]. Physically, if two boats race 13 times in the America's Cup Match, the same two AC75 hulls race every day — the syndicate does not draw a brand-new random boat design from the prior before Race 2, Race 3, ..., Race 13!
