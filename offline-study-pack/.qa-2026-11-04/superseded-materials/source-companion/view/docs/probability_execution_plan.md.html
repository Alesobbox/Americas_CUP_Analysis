7## 1. Fixed product contract
8
9Implement exactly three user-facing outputs:
10
111. **Race win at time t:** probability of being the first valid on-water finisher, using only information available by the prediction cutoff. Subsequent protests or result changes are outside this target.
122. **Pre-race expected position:** mean simulated on-water finishing position for the entered field and conditions. DNS/DNF/RET receive position **N**, where N is the scheduled field size. Label the card **Expected position · nonfinish scored N**. This deliberately penalized last-place-equivalent metric is not an official points score or mid-rank, and its rank sum need not be conserved. Preserve the user's chosen convention; do not add a fourth finish-probability output. Calculate the mid-rank alternative only in the audit report to show the convention's impact.
133. **2027 America's Cup win:** probability of winning the actual AC75 trophy through the full official competition structure. Apply official scoring and completed results here, separately from the on-water target in output 1.
14
15Keep a static dashboard with Python-generated artifacts. Common pre-race conditions select prebuilt supported presets immediately; off-grid/custom conditions require a build. Include external weather. Do not add podium, advancement, source-regatta winner, or other probability cards. Internal ranking distributions, completion probabilities and advancement states are necessary machinery, not additional products.
16
17Default race forecasts are knowledge-time forecasts, not hindsight reconstructions trained on later races. A current prospective scenario can use all eligible history available at its cutoff. Scenario changes apply prospectively from that cutoff; they cannot rewrite a historical start or legal gate already completed.

35## 3. Research-backed architecture
36
37```mermaid
38flowchart TD
39 A[Raw timestamped sources and reviewed labels] --> B[Causal snapshots and training tables]
40 W[Archived weather and scenario conditions] --> B
41 B --> C[Strength, performance and completion components]
42 C --> D[Correlated Monte Carlo race engine]
43 D --> E[Race win and expected position]
44 D --> F[Versioned AC75 adapter and official tournament rules]
45 F --> G[2027 Cup win]
46 E --> H[Static versioned artifacts]
47 G --> H
48 V[Chronological evaluation and promotion gates] --> C
49```
50
51Monte Carlo is the common integration and outcome-generation method. It is not a substitute for estimating realistic distributions. Compare a direct ranking sampler with an empirical course simulator using the same evidence, completion model, weather scenarios and evaluation.
52
53The leading generative candidate simulates **next-gate and future-leg times**, not invented future GPS tracks or optimal tactics. The direct yacht simulation paper [R01](forecasting_research_review.md#r01--joint-yacht-race-simulation) motivates joint weather/performance; the missing physical inputs make a full VPP/digital twin an inappropriate v1 dependency.
54
55All displayed outputs for a given class/scenario must derive from the same versioned joint outcome model. Highest win probability does not imply best expected position: even a coherent joint distribution can trade frequent wins against frequent last places. Test internal distributional consistency, not equality of these rankings. If the direct ranking model beats the course simulator, use its Monte Carlo finishing-order sampler and label it accurately. Model complexity is not an acceptance criterion.

87### P2 — Build the causal data path and condition scenarios
88
89**Raw event path:** preserve original record order, source event time, record ordinal, and receipt/publication time when available. Use an event-time reorder buffer of six seconds, matching the observed 5.85-second disorder with a margin. When receipt times are absent, call this an event-time reconstruction with modeled latency, not proof of live information availability. Late events beyond the allowance are flagged; do not silently insert them into earlier published checkpoints.
90
91**Snapshots/state quality:** construct inputs at 1 Hz; publish every ten seconds and on definitive finish/status changes. Two seconds becomes a **fresh-measurement label**, not an automatic fleet-wide failure threshold. A causal position/velocity filter propagates a posterior state during short gaps, with uncertainty increasing under its full dynamics. Sample that uncertainty once in Monte Carlo; mark the input `estimated`, with age and covariance, never measured. Use the filter and certification procedure in annex A. No future interpolation endpoint or smoother is permitted.
92
93Fit noise parameters from earlier data and certify gap horizons using disjoint artificial masks at 2/5/10/20/30/60/90 seconds, including turns, low speed and gate approaches. Select only the largest contiguous horizon passing the preregistered coverage/error/phase tests; 60 or 90 seconds is a ceiling to test, not an assumed safe bridge. Recreate the natural 74.6-second case as a stress test, without promising a valid result. At a possible unconfirmed gate crossing, keep discrete course-state uncertainty; never mark a predicted crossing as observed. MVP only bridges paths whose legal phase remains resolved. For longer/ambiguous gaps, use a missing-state prior only if its own masked-gap end-to-end evaluation passes; otherwise return an explicit unresolved-field-state result. Retain the competitor in all cases; never renormalize it away.
94
95Marks use a separate slow-motion/random-walk model or causal hold with age-dependent covariance, initially capped at 30 seconds and shortened by masked-mark validation. Course changes, relocations and uncertain gate lines stop blind holding. Use trailing windows of 10/30/120 seconds and coverage. A fresh official wind asset can replace unavailable local wind with provenance. First-finisher determinism requires globally resolved chronological finish evidence, not merely the first received crossing while another contender's state is unknown.
96
97**Course state:** consume only revisions known by the cutoff. Derive starts, legal gates and progress online. Use metre geometry, correctly directed leg VMG and actual marks. Add along-wind and cross-wind offsets to both gate endpoints/center using a causal circular trailing wind vector, gate angular width and current tack. Do not hardcode 42°/145° polars or the review's invalid distance formula. These are geometric features, not a verified layline or feasible route. Existing full-race progress/manoeuvre outputs remain retrospective. Add causal SOG, 10-second acceleration, turn-rate/time-since-turn, speed-relative-to-earlier-steady-speed and wrapped 30-versus-120-second wind shift. Threshold flags are explicitly proxies; SOG<11 knots does not establish displacement/foil state.
98
99**Tables:** build one compact table per boat-leg for full-leg targets; a separate ten-second snapshot table for remaining-time and winner targets; a 1 Hz/window table for sequence experiments. Carry race/day/leg IDs and validity masks. Overlapping rows within a boat-leg share total weight one; evaluate at the race/day level. Gate times after the cutoff may be target labels, never predictor inputs.
100
101**Weather:** use Open-Meteo archived ECMWF IFS individual runs for retrospective exogenous forecasts and forecast ensembles where available for prospective scenarios. Cache exact model identifiers, run initialization, conservative/verified availability, valid times, coordinates, height, units and response hashes. Do not substitute reanalysis or realized wind as a past forecast. For dates beyond useful forecast horizons, use 1991–2020 Naples seasonal/daytime weather blocks as a documented climatological scenario, not a dated 2027 prediction. Preserve adjacent-race dependence.
102
103Manual scenarios specify field, class, venue, start time, wind speed/direction and optional course/variability overrides. Manual wind overrides provider wind for that variable; omitted variables retain the named provider baseline. Provider marine waves/current are context until supported effects can be estimated. External 10 m wind is a proxy, not the committee's sensor/current-corrected measurement. Out-of-range joint wind/course/class combinations are flagged as extrapolation; unsupported classes/geometry produce unavailable output.
104
105**Gate:** full-source and source-prefix builds agree for the same knowledge cutoff. Altering/deleting future telemetry, future statuses, final metadata or future results cannot change earlier inputs/forecasts. Raw source measurements and publication assumptions remain traceable. No actual future wind, heading, tack, next-gate choice or revised course is passed as a known-future covariate.

129### P4 — Implement mandatory simple references and completion
130
131**References:** field-aware uniform order sampler; pooled empirical historical order/leg sampler; static regularized strength model; a condition/state-aware conditional logit/Plackett–Luce model with a small penalized additive extension for progress. Use a shared competition normalization, not independent binary win classifiers. Keep a TrueSkill-style rating as a diagnostic benchmark rather than stacking several ratings by default.
132
133The ranking score uses centered organization/entry effects, strongly pooled wind-response differences, recent relative VMG and a small geometry set. Add the diffusion-inspired dimensionless lead feature `lead_fraction / sqrt(max(remaining_fraction,0.01))`, comparing it with raw lead as a fixed ablation. It is motivated by a Brownian remaining-time approximation, not a universal first-passage theorem for foiling boats. Residual persistence, different speeds and shared wind can change the scaling. Known resolved finishes remain deterministic, avoiding a boundary singularity. A shared additive weather term cancels in a softmax. With two match races, pool evidence and do not fit a free matchup matrix.
134
135Use standardized features, ridge penalties and a single preregistered conservative configuration. For the initial regularized ranking fit, use strength SD 0.5, condition-response SD 0.25 and state-coefficient SD 0.25 on standardized inputs; include a zero-condition-effect ablation. These are declared regularization conventions, not measured physical constants. Do not estimate free daily skill paths. Compare a fixed 180-day recency sensitivity only after the static reference is recorded; it is not an automatic learned drift model.
136
137**Completion:** separate scheduled-entry DNS from terminal exogenous noncompletion after starting, such as an audited retirement/capsize/breakdown. Race time limits, finishing windows and administrative abandonment are handled by the rules/time model, not counted again as random reliability failures. An untyped DNF requires review; do not guess its cause. Fit only audited competitive ascertainment; do not include missing streams, practice stopping, or abandonment as failures. Use pooled Beta-Binomial components with a declared Jeffreys reference prior and a Beta(1,19) prior-sensitivity run. If no audited denominator exists, mark unconditional predictions unavailable; do not present a prior mean as empirical reliability. Do not learn per-team failure rates from a handful of incidents.
138
139Reference failure risk uses constant hazard per course fraction, excludes DNS after a known start, and conditions on observed survival. Add a prespecified transition-weighted sensitivity whose phase weights integrate to the same full-race failure probability (annex D). With six raw RETIRED statuses and unaudited timing, do not claim fitted gate-specific hazards. The reliability component is otherwise independent of sampled ability/performance; slowness-induced failure still arises from timing rules. Preserve terminal statuses only from availability time. Legacy `elapsed_s=0` for nonfinishers is a sentinel, never a zero-second finish.
140
141Fit ranking on the established valid finisher order; do not also encode DNF as poor strength. For conditional travel-time learning, retain every observed valid gate transition, including transitions by a boat that later fails to finish; future failure must not delete its earlier physical evidence. Terminal noncompletion remains in the separate completion component. Retain the possibility of no valid finisher internally. Unknown future penalties/rulings are not a fabricated learned hazard: expose the event scenario as conditional on no unmodeled future scoring sanctions.
142
143**Gate:** hand-checkable examples establish coherent ranking probabilities, terminal behavior and the N convention. Confirm no double counting of DNS/DNF, and that win mass plus no-finisher mass equals one. Expected positions lie in [1,N], but need not sum to N(N+1)/2 when nonfinishers tie at N.

145### P5 — Build the empirical correlated race simulator
146
147**Positive time models:** compare the small lognormal/additive reference with a pooled positive-delay mixture: `T_leg = T_clean + Z*delay`, where `T_clean` is positive, Z is an episode indicator and delay is positive **seconds**. Fit the mixture jointly against the same baseline; do not append delays to a full-leg model already containing those losses. Audit sustained loss/recovery episodes with retrospective manoeuvre information only as training labels, retaining their detection/availability semantics. The source has no reliable foil-height state: call the component a severe performance-loss episode unless independently validated as touchdown. No fixed 9-knot wind switch, 11-knot foil classifier or 25–60-second delay is adopted as fact. Use the fit/identifiability/fallback specification in annex C.
148
149The current remaining-gate model uses cutoff distance/progress, wind, trailing speed/VMG, acceleration/recovery proxies and observed geometry. Future full legs use only leg attributes, sampled calendar-time weather, hierarchical performance and simulated gate-time gaps, never ungenerated future speed/position. Fit crew effects once on the time scale; do not add an independent copy of ranking worth. An audited time-limit observation may contribute the appropriate censored likelihood; retirement is a separate event. Source-truncated targets remain unknown unless censoring is established.
150
151**Gate consistency:** use one shared leg-level prior and persistent pace/loss variables for full-leg and remaining-leg predictions. A remaining-leg conditional update with zero elapsed progress and no new information must recover the full-leg predictive distribution, including mixture mass, mean, variance and tails. Do not independently redraw a leg's latent loss state at a gate. Test continuity with new observations held fixed, and separately permit justified updates from observed rounding/recovery/conditions. Do not smooth away real information-driven probability jumps or enforce unconditional equal variance after informative observations.
152
153**Starts:** pre-race simulations draw a signed joint gun-time state: distance/position relative to the line, speed, and valid/corrected-start status, using reviewed competitive starts with strong pooling. Include poor legal starts and corrected OCS cases. First-leg arrival time is defined from the gun and conditions on this sampled state; do not add another sampled start delay to an already gun-to-gate duration. DNS remains separate.
154
155**Joint evolution:** for each whole simulated race, draw one persistent crew/entry effect, one race-performance effect, a shared weather trajectory and centered residual innovations. Model a shared fleet/day component plus boat-specific persistence across legs. Estimate dependence with small factors/shrinkage and synchronized residual blocks, not a full unrestricted boat-by-time covariance matrix. Prefer strictly earlier out-of-fold residuals. For early folds without those residuals, use empirical same-phase blocks from earlier training races and flag the dependence estimate as provisional. Do not add an independent second crew effect or a second copy of weather variance already explained by the sampled weather.
156
157**Transitions:** maintain an event queue of sampled gate arrivals and accumulate each boat's calendar time. At a new leg, first compute the deterministic median duration using weather at entry; summarize the shared sampled weather over that baseline window, then draw the conditional duration. Recompute after every gate. Different boats need not encounter the same wind merely because their leg numbers match. Use course revisions only if already known; future course changes belong to explicit scenarios. When a boat arrives, form the time-lead feature at the latest gate already reached by both boats, or use zero plus an unknown flag; never inspect future sampled arrivals as tactical information. Restrict spatial proximity effects to the observed current segment. Test the time-lead effect against a no-interaction ablation. These are empirical effects, not an optimal covering policy or a collision/umpire model.
158
159Sample a terminal-failure course fraction from the assumed hazard, retaining a boat's competitive presence until that fraction rather than revealing its future retirement at the gun. Interpolate event time within its sampled leg solely to schedule that failure; this is an exposure approximation, not a predicted XY track. Apply each race's verified first-gate/race/finishing-window rules separately, retaining reached gates and status for official scoring. Do not apply a time-limit DNF once through reliability and again through the timing rule.
160
161Full-leg times already include ordinary manoeuvre/tactical losses. Do not add a separate tack-cost process on top without decomposing and refitting the baseline. Do not extrapolate precise future XY paths from a gate-time model or animate them as predictions.
162
163**Monte Carlo/build cost:** use common random numbers. Standard live checkpoints use 20,000 continuations, with 100,000 reserved for pre-race final artifacts and explicitly built Cup scenarios. Report numerical precision; worst-case approximate 95% half-width is 0.69 percentage points at 20,000 and 0.31 at 100,000, not model accuracy. Benchmark one complete race and one Cup scenario before bulk generation. Batch/vectorize, cache by full input/model/seed hash, and reuse identical builds. Canonical weather presets initially generate pre-race summaries only, not a Cartesian expansion of every ten-second live trajectory. Build custom live scenarios lazily. Keep simulation counts fixed in each declared build profile; do not spend weeks training models before this cost gate.
164
165**Finite-sample tails:** before scoring or export, define the joint predictive distribution as `(1 - 0.0001) * candidate + 0.0001 * valid_uniform_reference`. The reference uses the same completion component and legal/terminal constraints, with uniform order among possible finishers; calculate its win/place marginals analytically or by exact finisher-subset enumeration. Apply the same mixture to expected positions and tournament outcome sampling. This predeclared small regularization prevents a possible winner receiving a numerical zero solely from finite Monte Carlo counts. Preserve truly impossible/terminal outcomes exactly; never independently clip displayed percentages after observing a surprise.
166
167**Gate:** label swaps, equal-strength symmetry, finish determinism, legal gate order, fixed known results, missing-data behavior, and dependence ablations pass. The simulator is compared with P4 end to end; plausible dynamics alone do not justify promotion.

169### P6 — Optional shallow ML; other additions are post-MVP
170
171**Shallow boosting:** one CatBoost residual model, depth 2, 300 iterations, learning rate 0.03, L2 leaf penalty 10, squared-error loss on standardized log-time residuals, seed 42 and no broad tuning. Use explicit past-fitted strength features and avoid target-encoding leakage from team IDs. Compare it with the additive simulator on identical rows/splits/seeds. Nonlinear mean gains do not automatically imply calibrated uncertainty; refit current-segment residuals after the correction and retain the common dependence machinery.
172
173**Phase 2 only — SVR:** one RBF residual comparator, with inputs and log-time target standardized from training data, C=1, epsilon=0.1 and gamma=`scale`, on a deterministic race-balanced subset of at most 20,000 training windows. Do not delay core shipping for this experiment.
174
175**Phase 2 only — unsupervised:** compare no regime model with a two-state HMM on trailing wind/performance summaries. Fit only the training fold and use filtering, never smoothing. Known leg state comes from metadata. Anomalies create review flags, not retirement labels. The MVP uses continuous causal kinematic features from P2; it does not require an HMM to explain obvious turn/recovery patterns.
176
177**Gate:** every addition has a recorded ablation and a bounded experiment. No new feature/model is added merely because the library exposes it. Failure to improve is an acceptable result.

179### P7 — Post-MVP deep/foundation research; never a shipping gate
180
181Begin only after P0–P5/P8–P9 pass core verification and error analysis identifies short-horizon residual structure not explained by SOG, acceleration, turn/recovery and wind-shift features. The existing data cannot establish a large model's value in advance. The review's specific claim that 95% of its signal is redundant is also unmeasured; test incremental value rather than assert it. Preserve the bounded experiment below as research, not required MVP work.
182
183**Frozen models first:** compare Chronos-2 grouped multivariate, TimesFM 2.5 univariate/XReg, and the simple persistence/recent-average/AR references. Chronos-2 is the default multivariate local experiment because its official weights are Apache-2.0. 191**Gate-time adapter:** let `m0` be the baseline predicted log remaining-gate time, `L` the known current-leg length and `D` its causal remaining along-course distance proxy. Set H to the smaller of 30 seconds and `exp(m0)`; define baseline median progress over H as `D * min(H / exp(m0), 1)`. Form `a = (sequence median cumulative progress over H - baseline median cumulative progress over H) / L` and `b = sequence interdecile cumulative-progress width over H / L`. Train on past cross-fitted forecasts to predict `log(T_remaining)` with mean `m0 + gamma*a` and log residual scale `log(sigma0) + zeta*b`, using ridge penalty 10 on standardized a/b, gamma<=0 and zeta>=0. If no eligible adapter-training forecasts exist, return the baseline distribution. Integrate 1 Hz rate forecasts to obtain cumulative progress; interpolate the compact neural head between its known anchors (0,10,30,60 seconds). Do not use actual future gate time to choose H. This is a fitted summary-to-arrival adapter, not integration of a physical future track.

Short-horizon effects have value only through this fitted, evaluated adapter.

260## 6. Acceptance and stopping rules
261
262- **Data correctness comes first:** no supervised performance claim without audited target evidence; no new semantic use of existing `official_rank` compatibility fields.
263- **Causality:** future perturbation/prefix equivalence passes for features, strengths, completion, exogenous inputs and exported checkpoint selection.
264- **Coherence:** complete joint outcomes yield both race metrics and official event scoring; no independently fitted dashboard percentages, no missing-opponent normalization, no doubled failure/manoeuvre risk.
265- **Predictive value:** report competitive out-of-time scores and each date's contribution. A simpler model remains a successful release if challengers fail. No invented target accuracy or promised percentage improvement.
266- **Uncertainty:** distinguish race randomness, parameter sensitivity, external weather, AC75 transfer, rule ambiguity and numerical Monte Carlo error. Do not label a prior/scenario envelope a calibrated confidence interval.
267- **Missing evidence:** unavailable states are a correct implementation result for that input; list the exact evidence that would enable the output. Implement unaffected components rather than filling gaps with constants.
268- **Reproducibility:** same sources/configuration/model/seed produce identical artifacts, with deterministic tie handling only where official rules specify it.
269- **Release safety:** all existing replay/historical checks pass; only the promoted and correctly licensed model bundle enters production artifacts. Experiments remain discoverable in Reports/model cards without silently changing the model in use.
