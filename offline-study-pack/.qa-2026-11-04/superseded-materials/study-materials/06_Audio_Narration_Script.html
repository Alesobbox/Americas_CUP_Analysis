Act I — What Problem Does This System Solve, and How Is It Architected? (00:00 – 04:30)

Welcome to the offline audio walkthrough of the 2D_regatta_replay repository. If you are hiking or sitting in a cabin without Wi-Fi, this script walks you through the entire system from raw satellite telemetry to 60-frame-per-second browser rendering and causal tournament probability bounds.

At its core, this project solves three distinct analytical problems for America's Cup foiling regattas — problems that are often conflated in sports analytics, but which require completely different mathematical machinery:

  1. First: Retrospective Observation (Replay and Compare Workspaces). What physically happened on the water at every tenth of a second? Where were the nine AC40 foiling monohulls and the eight autonomous robotic course marks (RM1 through RM8), when did each boat's bowsprit cross the starting line or gate line, when did they tack or gybe, and where did their umpiring keep-clear zones overlap?
  2. Second: Retrospective Performance Evaluation (Analysis and Reports Workspaces). Once a race or regatta is finished, how fast was a boat relative to the peers that actually sailed the same legs in the same wind? Why did a team lose twenty seconds on Leg 3 — was it straight-line upwind VMG, a bad wind header, or speed loss in a tack?
  3. Third: Causal Forward Prediction (Forecast Workspace). Using only information legally available up to a strict historical cutoff timestamp, what is each boat's expected finishing position before the gun (pre-race-pl-mc-1), what is each boat's live probability of winning the race from its current on-water position (live-mc-kalman-1), and what can — and cannot — be said about the 2027 38th America's Cup in AC75s (cup-ac38-conditional-2)?

To keep these three worlds from contaminating one another, the repository enforces one cardinal architectural boundary: Python owns all historical metric formulas and all forecasting models. TypeScript owns only display-rate interpolation, live instantaneous geometry, and the user interface. The browser never recalculates a historical metric, never re-runs a Plackett–Luce regression, and never fabricates a fallback probability when an artifact is marked unavailable.

Act II — What Information Actually Exists in the Archive? (04:30 – 08:30)

Before trusting any equation, you have to know the exact shape of the raw archive. Inside Data/, there are 28 raw .jsonl session files totaling 6.87 gigabytes (6,866,006,601 bytes). Those 28 sessions span 9 race days across two 2026 AC40 preliminary events: - Cagliari, Sardinia (May 22 to May 24, 2026): 9 sessions (63 boat-races). - Naples, Italy (September 22 to September 27, 2026): 19 sessions (161 replayed boat-races, 164 scheduled boat-races).

Why do we constantly distinguish 224 replayed boat-races from 227 scheduled boat-races? Because on September 22 in Naples, in Practice Races 2, 3, and 4 (26_09_22_nap_ipr_pr2, pr3, and pr4), GBR B was listed in the scheduled race roster (race_start_status), but transmitted zero boat_state telemetry packets. During replay ingestion (pipeline/ingest.py, lines 699 to 700), boats with empty telemetry lists are dropped so the 2D canvas doesn't render phantom boats at (0, 0) — leaving 224 replayed boat-races in manifests.json and analysis/index.json. However, the forecasting pipeline (pipeline/prediction/labels.py) restores all 227 scheduled starts in .forecast/scheduled_entry_ledger.json, because dropping a scheduled DNS entrant would bias completion rates upward and shrink the field size N used in tied-N scoring.

Even more importantly: across all 28 sessions, official_order_verified is False. We do not have signed race-committee finishing sheets or umpire penalty logs. Every finishing rank in the archive (RANK_1, RANK_2, DNF, OCS) is reconstructed from telemetry and autonomous mark roundings. And while the synchronized replays contain 331,089 frames at 100-millisecond spacing, 331,089 frames do not mean 331,089 independent observations! For cross-race team skill or weather effects, your sample size is 28 sessions across 9 calendar days.

Act III — How Raw Telemetry Becomes a 10 Hz Replay (08:30 – 13:00)

Let's trace how a 250-megabyte .jsonl file becomes a compact replay JSON in public/data/replays/. 1. Projection (pipeline/projection.py): Raw GNSS latitudes and longitudes are projected onto a local tangent plane in metres East (x) and metres North (y) using WGS84 ellipsoidal curvature coefficients (klat ≈ 111,048m/deg and klon ≈ 84,375m/deg at Naples; 86,408m/deg at Cagliari). 2. 10 Hz Synchronization (pipeline/synchronize.py): Boats transmit at roughly 10 Hz with clock jitter; robotic marks transmit at roughly 1 Hz. TimelineSynchronizer resamples every channel onto a uniform 100-millisecond grid. Scalar channels (x, y, sog, vmg, tws, twa) use linear interpolation. Angular bearings (heading, cog, twd) use shortest-arc circular interpolation modulo 360 degrees: Δθ = ((θ1 - θ0 + 540^∘) \bmod 360^∘) - 180^∘. If a boat stops transmitting early — like GBR B in naples_pr1, which retires 15 minutes and 51 seconds before the window ends — its coordinates are clamped to its final packet, creating a "held tail" that historical_analysis.py explicitly excludes. 3. Bow-Tip Crossing Geometry (pipeline/reconstruction.py): An AC40's GNSS receiver sits at the transom centerline (0, 0), while the bowsprit tip sits +12.963metres forward along the boat's true heading ψ. At every 100-millisecond step, CourseReconstructor projects the bow tip pbow(t) = (x + 12.963sinψ, y + 12.963cosψ) and solves the 2D cross-product segment intersection u, v ∈ [0, 1] against the start line or active gate segment. If a boat rounds slightly outside a mark or if one gate mark is missing, it falls back to a 200-metre virtual gate corridor (MAX_GATE_RADIUS_M = 100.0). Cumulative race progress progress_m is then computed and clamped with a running maximum so progress_m is strictly non-decreasing. 4. Manoeuvres & Virtual Contacts (pipeline/manoeuvres.py, pipeline/analytics.py): Because raw manoeuvres records are empty (0 records), ManoeuvreDetector scans 10 Hz telemetry for a 4-gate signature: smoothed turn rate ≥ 8^∘/s, opposite-sign entry/exit TWA in [20^∘, 160^∘], heading change ≥ 32^∘, and speed loss ≥ 2.5kt. Meanwhile, detect_virtual_contacts transforms each boat's 9-vertex keep_clear_border (± 7.415m) and platform_border (± 5.415m) into world space and runs the Separating Axis Theorem (SAT) at 10 Hz, coalescing overlaps separated by ≤ 2.0s into incidents.

Act IV — Historical Metrics and the Peer-Normalized Radar (13:00 – 17:30)

In pipeline/historical_analysis.py (schema_version = "2.0", formula_version = "historical-2"), every race, leg, and manoeuvre metric is paired with coverage metadata and a strict quality badge (derived requires ≥ 95% valid source-second coverage).

Two subtleties in Part IV are essential for your oral defense: 1. First: The VMG Sign Convention. In raw replay telemetry, vmg is wind-referenced (SOGcos(TWA)), which is negative whenever a boat sails downwind (|TWA| > 90^∘). In historical_analysis.py, leg VMG (avg_vmg_kt, upwind_vmg, downwind_vmg) is mark-referenced along the active leg vector, so progress toward the leeward gate is positive! 2. Second: The 6-Axis Self-Excluding Peer Radar (performance-1). To compare boats fairly when wind speed changes across legs or when some boats DNF early, _boat_performance restricts comparison to matched_legs — legs where both the subject boat and at least one peer have valid derived metrics. On each matched leg \ell, the baseline x-b,\ell is the mean of all valid peers excluding boat b itself, and the leg index is Ib,\ell = 100 · xb,\ell / x-b,\ell. The dimension index Ib,d is the unweighted mean of per-leg indices. Because the average of ratios is not the ratio of averages (12(x2y2 + x4y4) ≠ x2+x4y2+y4), ITA G's Downwind VMG index in naples_pr1 is 109.9671, whereas 100 * value / peer_mean is 108.0853.

Act V — The Three Causal Forecasting Systems (17:30 – 22:30)

Now let's examine pipeline/prediction/. Why do we say there are three separate forecasting targets? 1. Target 1: Pre-Race Expected Finish Position (pre-race-pl-mc-1): Predicts expected finish rank 𝔼[Ri] ∈ [1, N] across all scheduled starters under the tied-N nonfinish rule, where every non-finisher (DNS, DNF, OCS, RET) receives worst rank N. When M boats fail to finish, the sum of ranks is N(N+1)2 + M(M-1)2, so expected positions sum to strictly more than N(N+1)2 whenever two or more boats can fail to finish! 2. Target 2: Live In-Race Win Probability (live-mc-kalman-1 / correlated_mc): Every 10 seconds during a race, a 6-second ReorderBuffer feeds a 2D Constant-Velocity Kalman Filter (CVKalmanFilter), publishing at published_at_ms = state_timestamp_ms + 6000. The promoted correlated_mc simulator samples remaining gate times using a persistent crew factor, shared calendar-time weather noise, and remaining-distance-scaled boat innovations, cutting live Brier score by 45% (0.8387 down to 0.4534) compared to static Plackett–Luce. 3. Target 3: The 2027 America's Cup (cup-ac38-conditional-2 in cup_2027.json): Simulates the 7-team 2027 AC38 tournament in Naples (6 fleet races with 10..4 points and carry-over, 3 Round Robins, Repechages, first-to-5 Semifinals and Final, and first-to-7 Cup Match against Defender ETNZ). Crucially, team capability is drawn once per tournament trial and held persistent across all matches (preventing the i.i.d. series law-of-large-numbers distortion that inflates a 75.00% mixture to 93.76%). And because 1,723 of 5,000 baseline trials (34.46%) encounter ties in the 6-Challenger fleet carry or Round Robin seeding where RRSAC 102.1 tie-break procedures are unpublished, headline point probabilities are null, and each team is reported with an exact conservative rule-bound interval [lower, upper] of width 0.3446 (34.46 percentage points).

Act VI — Verification, Audit Findings & Final Takeaway (22:30 – 25:00)

When we ran .\run.ps1 verify on 2026-11-04, all 828 deterministic tests passed (406 frontend Vitest tests, 291 Python unit tests, 123 E2E Tier 1–4 tests, and 8 Tier 5 white-box integrity tests), alongside cryptographic SHA-256 verification of all published forecast and environment artifacts.

At the same time, our code audit uncovered six concrete implementation nuances you can cite from memory: 1. pipeline/reconstruction.py:L228 checks top-level comp.get("started") instead of comp.get("start_state", {}).get("started"), causing USA in naples_pr1 to be reconstructed as RANK_4 before historical_analysis.py flags it as conflict. 2. pipeline/analytics.py:L263 divides metre lead gaps by constant 8.0 m/s, whereas src/engine/analytics.ts:L71 divides by the leader's live sog. 3. pipeline/manoeuvres.py uses 2.5 kt speed dip and 90% recovery vs its 3.0 kt docstring and 95% recovery in historical_analysis.py. 4. 26_09_22_nap_ipr_pr2 has a non-monotonic source Leg 3 timestamp for USA, causing overlapping leg coverage (1,036.3 s vs 865.4 s elapsed). 5. tactical_insights.py suppresses 100% (0 / 3,455) of 5-minute wind projections due to strict R2 ≥ 0.55 and split-half linearity gates. 6. PositiveDelayMixture is fully coded in models.py but gated off (available = False) because 0 human-reviewed clean/loss leg labels exist.
