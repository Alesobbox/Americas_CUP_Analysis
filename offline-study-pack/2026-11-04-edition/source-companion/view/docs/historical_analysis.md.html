1# Historical race analysis
2
3## Artifacts and lifecycle
4
5`run.ps1 ingest` processes every `Data/*.jsonl` file, upserts its replay into `public/data/manifests.json`, writes `public/data/analysis/<race-id>.json`, then rebuilds `public/data/analysis/index.json` from the catalog. The detail and index use schema `2.0` and method `historical-2`. Repeating ingestion with the same sources yields the same statistics. The index contains compact boat-race results, start outcomes, completed-leg measures for the source course's four or six legs, and manoeuvre distributions with originating race counts, but no 10 Hz telemetry. The browser loads the index when analysis opens and fetches a race detail only when that race is selected. New catalog races with a matching analysis artifact enter the history automatically.
6
7Python defines the metric catalog in `pipeline/historical_analysis.py:DEFINITIONS`; the exporter embeds it in every detail artifact. The TypeScript analysis view consumes those definitions and values and performs no metric calculations. Each scalar value carries its unit, quality, covered seconds, and an unavailability reason where applicable. Quality `derived` means at least 95% of the requested active interval passed the source timestamp checks; `partial` means some samples were available but coverage fell below 95%. An empty source or invalid window produces null with a reason. `reconstructed` identifies detector or checkpoint outputs. The historical median/range excludes partial and unavailable observations and counts each boat-race once. Event distributions use individual manoeuvres and show counts.
8
9## Compact event feed
10
11Each race detail has an additive `event_feed` object with `schema_version: "1.0"`, `method_version: "event-feed-1"`, and timestamp-sorted `events[]`. An event has a stable `id`, `timestamp_ms` (Unix milliseconds), `type`, `label`, `source`, and `quality`; boat events also have the source asset `boat_id`. `unavailable_reason` is optional for future event types. The feed selects the session gun, reconstructed bow-line start crossings, source-timed or geometrically reconstructed gate crossings, detected tacks and gybes, and finish crossings only when a ranked boat has a final-leg gate timestamp. Missing crossings are omitted rather than assigned a gun, window-end, or other fallback time. Source start-state conflicts are marked `source_conflict`; `source_timing` on a gate means a source leg-time record, not an independently verified official result.
12
13Wind-shift events use the bounded official-wind samples already exported in `wind.timeline`, not local boat wind or a visual estimate. Python emits a shift when the shortest circular change from the last emitted reference reaches 10° and at least 30 seconds have passed. The event timestamp is the selected official sample time, its quality is `measured_sample`, and no wind event is emitted without a qualifying sample. This is a descriptive change in reported direction, not evidence of a tactical cause.
14
15## Timing, provenance, and coverage
16
17The source GNSS, heading, COG, SOG, and boat wind streams are measured at different rates. Ingestion projects GNSS to local metres, converts speeds to knots, and interpolates on the 100 ms replay grid. Analysis uses those unrounded synchronized arrays and source timestamp coverage. It rejects samples before the first source timestamp, after the last timestamp, or inside a source gap longer than two seconds. Leg intervals are clipped at the actual reconstructed crossing timestamps rather than at the nearest 100 ms frame. The existing replay's rounded telemetry and fixed-axis VMG remain compatible, but neither is used as a rounded input to the new calculations.
18
19The source does **not** provide independently verified official finishing classifications for most boats. The supplied leg timing records and geometric fallbacks determine the exported rankings and elapsed times. The keys `official_rank` and `official_elapsed_s` remain in the schema for compatibility; the interface labels them Result and Elapsed, with a note that they are calculated from recorded telemetry and have not been independently verified. Both are null for DNF/RET. Display wording does not change internal quality codes or source provenance. USA's PR1 nested `start_state.started` is false even though the reconstruction assigns a start and finish. That conflict is flagged for adjudication. A DNF has no finish time, and a RET/OCS boat without a valid start has no active-race performance score.

21## Metric contract
22
23The artifact's `definitions` object is the machine-readable contract for each scalar: label, unit, formula, window, denominator, and source. The main calculations are:
24
25| Group | Definition and availability |
26| --- | --- |
27| Course VMG | `SOG × cos(COG − leg bearing)` in knots. Odd legs use the course axis; even legs use axis + 180°. Mean and time-weighted 10th/90th percentiles are per leg. The replay's `vmg` remains a signed fixed-axis trace and is never averaged across the whole course for performance. |
28| Wind VMG | `SOG × cos(COG − boat TWD)` on upwind legs and its negative on downwind legs. TWD is the direction the wind comes **from**. Mean and percentiles require valid local wind timestamps. |
29| True wind angle | Signed shortest difference `TWD − heading` remains in replay. Analysis uses `|TWA|` for distributions and separate port/starboard stable-period medians. It does not average signed port and starboard TWA. |
30| Sailed distance | Sum of projected GNSS segment lengths in the valid active interval, clipped at exact start/gate/finish times. Missing source intervals are excluded, with covered seconds and quality reported. |
31| Route ratio | Straight distance between a leg's observed endpoint positions divided by its sailed distance. This geometric reference is **not** a feasible route through marks or gates. It is unavailable without both endpoints and a valid path. |
32| Tack allocation | Signed TWA above +15° is starboard, below −15° is port. A ±3 s window around detected manoeuvres and angles close to head-to-wind are excluded. Time and path distance are reported by leg and side. |
33| Manoeuvres | Reuses the detector's tack/gybe events, entry/minimum speed, and speed loss. Recovery is first sampled SOG reaching 95% of entry speed after the local minimum, within 15 s. Missing recovery is null, never zero. Acceleration is `(0.95 × entry − minimum) / recovery seconds`; events within 5 s of a gate are flagged ambiguous. Counts and medians are separated by event type. |
34| Boundary | Minimum signed distance from the boat centre to the **race** polygon active at the sample timestamp, positive inside. Time in the 0–25 m buffer and time outside are sampled exposure, not a rule decision. Outside periods lasting at least one second are linkable to replay. Safety polygon and boat Keep Clear border are different geometries and are not substituted. |
35| Start | Bow signed distance to the moving source sequence-0 port–starboard line at the gun; normalized along-line fraction 0 at port, 1 at starboard, and unclamped beyond ends. Cagliari uses RM3/RM7; Naples marks vary by session. Gun SOG and signed closing component use COG. OCS has no valid crossing order. |
36| Checkpoints | Each completed leg uses actual crossing timestamps. Gate split is the observed difference from the earliest valid crossing at that checkpoint. Split/rank change compares the boat's prior completed checkpoint. These are timestamp observations, distinct from replay's continuous progress time-gap estimate. |
37| Mark approach | Moving chosen mark at 20 s before crossing yields bearing and absolute COG-to-mark angle. Entry/exit SOG uses ±5 s. No stable rounding start/end event exists, so rounding duration is unavailable. |
38| Tactical separation | During overlapping active windows and matching legs, one-second samples yield median physical and lateral distance for each selected pair. They measure geometry, not race-progress advantage. |
39| Wind | Official roughly 1 Hz TWD shifts use the shortest circular difference from the first active official reading. Boat-reported local TWD is aligned to official samples only during valid active windows; deviations over 60° are excluded as outliers. Fleet spread requires at least two active valid boats. This is descriptive exposure, not proof of tactical causation. |

41## Boat performance and team debrief
42
43Each race detail additionally contains `boats[].performance`, schema `1.0`, method `performance-1`. The existing historical schema, historical scalars, event feed and replay format remain compatible. `attach_performance_analysis(detail)` deterministically derives the addition from the historical detail's existing metrics, crossing times, manoeuvres and source-coverage metadata; it does not fit a model or read rounded replay traces. The browser renders the exported values and never recalculates the scores.
44
45The six dimensions are `upwind_vmg`, `downwind_vmg`, `upwind_speed`, `downwind_speed`, `tack_retention`, and `gybe_retention`. A score of 100 means the mean of the **other eligible boats**, excluding the selected boat, on exactly the same completed leg in this race. For every matched leg, divide the selected boat's value by the equal-weight other-boats mean and multiply by 100. Average those leg scores with equal leg weights. The raw `index` is not capped; drawing limits do not change the exported score. `value` and `peer_mean` are the arithmetic means of the matched-leg raw values; their ratio need not equal the mean of the per-leg ratios.
46
47Directional dimensions use leg-directed course VMG or mean SOG in knots on odd (upwind) or even (downwind) legs. Each leg needs valid start/end crossings, a positive metric, quality `derived`, and source coverage of at least 95% of its elapsed interval. Incomplete, partial and unavailable legs cannot contribute. Boats with source start-state conflicts, OCS, retirement, DNS or no valid start are excluded both as subjects and peers. A DNF may still contribute its valid completed legs: a partial race is not represented as a full-race performance observation. Fleet and match sessions are compared only within their own race, with no archive pooling.
48
49Retention is `100 × minimum SOG / entry SOG` in percent for qualifying detected tacks or gybes. It describes the detector's selected events, which require at least 2.5 kt entry-to-minimum loss; it is not a distribution of all real manoeuvres. Events must have quality `reconstructed`, finite valid speeds, recorded loss at least 2.5 kt, and complete source SOG, heading and local TWD coverage throughout the detector's conservative window of 65 replay samples before to 60 samples after the event. At the default 100 ms cadence this is 6.5 s before to 6 s after; at 200 ms it is 13 s before to 12 s after. The detector searches 60 samples each side and reads entry speed up to five samples before turn onset. That whole window must lie inside the completed leg. Gapped, held, missing-source and ambiguous rounding events are excluded. Average events within each boat-leg, then give each other boat one vote in the peer mean, then average the matched-leg ratios. This prevents boats with more detected events receiving more weight as peers. Nondefault regular grids export `race.sample_interval_ms`; existing details without it retain the default 100 ms contract. Nonuniform or non-increasing replay grids are rejected rather than assigned a false cadence.
50
51Every dimension carries `id`, `label`, `index`, `value`, `peer_mean`, `unit`, `higher_is_better`, `peer_count`, `coverage_s`, `sample_count`, `matched_legs`, `quality`, `formula`, `window`, `denominator`, `source` and a reason when unavailable. `leg_evidence` retains the raw values, ratio, peer IDs/count, sample count and coverage for every matched leg. `peer_count` is the number of distinct other boats across matched legs. Directional `sample_count` counts qualifying completed legs, not telemetry frames; `coverage_s` sums their valid metric seconds. Retention `sample_count` counts qualifying events, while `coverage_s` measures the union of their cadence-scaled validated source windows within matched legs (12.5 s per isolated default-cadence event, 25 s at 200 ms), so overlapping windows do not double-count elapsed time. Debrief manoeuvre eligibility uses the same cadence-scaled coverage checks. An unavailable dimension has null values and an explicit reason. `overall_index` is the equal-weight mean of all six dimension scores only when all six are available; missing values are not imputed or dropped.
52
53`boats[].debrief`, schema `1.0`, method `debrief-1`, provides up to three largest positive completed-leg time deficits versus the other eligible boats' mean on the same leg, plus up to two largest covered, non-rounding detector speed reductions. Both the selected boat and every timing peer must have qualifying positive, at-least-95%-covered mean SOG for that completed leg; incomplete or partial-track legs are omitted. Each item supplies an ID, `kind`, title/detail, replay timestamp, observed value/unit, quality and source. Peer leg items also retain the peer IDs/count, baseline, interval and endpoint timing provenance (`source_timing` is not independently verified official timing). Replay links point to the checkpoint or manoeuvre timestamp. A leg deficit describes an observed duration difference; changing wind and different crossing times can contribute. A manoeuvre speed reduction does not estimate time or distance lost. These are retrospective review priorities and establish no causal attribution, calibrated ability estimate or polar efficiency.

55## Deferred and limited measures
56
57Measured target TWA, polar speed, and a reliable tactical baseline are absent, so estimated laylines are not exported. Bearing-to-moving-mark and approach angle remain available. Rounding duration and integrated manoeuvre distance loss have no stable event or baseline definition in these files. The historical-analysis exports contain 28 races and 224 replayed boat-races in separate fleet and match cohorts. The source files contain 227 competitor entries; GBR B has no replay telemetry in three 22 September Naples practice sessions, so those source entries do not become boat-race observations. Each race is one observation, not a telemetry-frame-weighted sample.
58
59The current ingestion maps known AC40 competitor and mark asset IDs from `pipeline/config.py`. Future files using new IDs or boat classes need a configuration update before they can be reconstructed, although any successfully ingested catalog race appears in the history automatically.

61## Verification
62
63The 3 November 2026 annotation revision preserves every historical scalar and performance-1 formula. Additive profile scope and per-leg peer/event evidence power the interactive chart; Reports and its CSVs use the same canonical metrics and reasons. The targeted `scripts/refresh_team_analytics.py --performance-only` command refreshes these outputs without reading raw source wind. See [team analytics](team_analytics.md) and [annotation revision verification](annotation_revision_verification.md).
64
65Use `run.ps1 verify` for the project's test/build workflow. `python -m unittest tests.unit.test_historical_analysis tests.unit.test_performance_analysis -v` covers hand-checkable formula and geometry cases, incomplete results, sparse wind, missing recovery, catalog boat-race counts, self-excluding matched-leg baselines, missing dimensions, source-window gaps and retrospective debrief priorities. Re-run `run.ps1 ingest` after changing metric formulas or adding source data, then inspect the regenerated index and selected race detail. The integrity suite checks all 28 replay and analysis paths, venue grouping, the six-leg Cagliari course, and the 22 September Naples benchmark.
